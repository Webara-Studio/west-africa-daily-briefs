## Deep Dive

### 1. Payment operations, not another wallet

PalmPay's reported emphasis on protection and dispute tracking shows incumbents already competing on trust. A generic “safer wallet” would face a difficult acquisition battle. The narrower hypothesis is a **merchant operations layer** that connects sales, settlement records and unresolved payments across existing providers.

Ghana's proposed GIIPS shared payment infrastructure for smaller lenders is a further watch item. Tech Labari reported on 1 September that it remained in procurement, with important cost and rollout details undisclosed. Do not design a pilot that depends on GIIPS being live.

### 2. Health administration has buyers — and competition

Tech Labari reported on 2 September that French insurer Alan acquired Senegal's Tanel, which operates in Senegal and Côte d’Ivoire. The article describes employer coverage management and a provider network, with further technology integration planned. Treat the transaction as reported rather than independently confirmed from closing documents.

The opportunity is not to assume these markets lack health apps. It is to find independent clinics or employer groups whose eligibility checks, supporting documents and claims follow-up remain expensive. A Ghana pilot would require separate local validation; Senegalese demand cannot simply be transplanted.

### 3. Agritech should follow a physical service

Disrupt Africa's 4 September profile of Wobil Technologies describes locally manufactured rice-processing machinery offered through pay-per-use and leasing models. Wobil reports serving 900 smallholder farmers since 2021 and operating in Ashanti and Volta. Those are company-reported operating figures, not independently audited totals.

This is a more concrete signal than a speculative farmer marketplace: equipment operators already have assets, customers and collections to coordinate. **Dispatch software is a proposed adjacent opportunity, not a verified gap in Wobil's own systems.**

## Commercial Opportunity

The following are **product hypotheses supported by adjacent signals**, not proven unserved markets. Revenue models are proposals; willingness to pay still needs testing.

| Product | Target buyer and revenue model | Local viability and first test |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Merchant reconciliation and dispute desk — first priority** | Ghanaian multi-branch retailers, distributors and service businesses; setup fee plus monthly subscription per outlet. | Link MoMo and bank/PSP records to invoices through authorised APIs or consented exports. Low-data dashboard, offline sales capture and SMS receipts. Pilot with five merchants; measure reconciliation time, unmatched payments and paid renewal. Nigeria requires its own bank-transfer integrations and partner checks. |
| **2. Clinic eligibility and claims workbench** | Independent provider groups and employer schemes in Dakar or Abidjan; onboarding plus provider subscription or contracted administration fee. | French-first interface, secure offline drafts and low-bandwidth synchronisation. Add Wolof audio guidance only after user testing. Integrate with authorised insurers; never confirm eligibility from stale offline data. Pilot with one provider group and measure rejected claims and administrative turnaround. |
| **3. Farm machinery dispatch and collections** | Rice-processing operators and cooperatives in Ashanti/Volta; operator subscription, with an optional disclosed fee per completed job. | Offline Android/PWA job cards, operator-assisted booking, MoMo receipts and Twi or Ewe voice prompts. Begin with one machinery operator; measure completed jobs, travel time, missed appointments and collection delays. Add USSD only if feature-phone usage and operator economics justify its recurring cost. |

**Execution recommendation:** sell a paid reconciliation pilot before funding a consumer wallet. Keep funds with licensed providers, obtain explicit data access, and make payment confirmation authoritative rather than screenshot-based. Price only after measuring the value saved and the cost of integrations, support, messaging and onboarding. For health products, sensitive-data safeguards and local insurance rules are launch gates; for agritech, asset availability and seasonal utilisation matter more than app downloads.

## Watch List

- **Seevcash:** reconcile the conflicting funding figures and verify card eligibility, country availability and fees directly before benchmarking.
- **Alan/Tanel:** verify the acquisition with company sources and watch provider integration requirements in Senegal and Côte d’Ivoire.
- **GIIPS:** track procurement and published integration access; it is not yet a dependable launch dependency.
- **Demand validation:** obtain current merchant/provider interviews and an accessible social sample. Today's Trends snapshot does not establish breakout demand for these products.
- **Wobil and similar operators:** investigate whether scheduling, maintenance and collections are genuinely painful before proposing software; do not build a marketplace before securing service capacity.
