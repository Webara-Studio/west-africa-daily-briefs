# 📈 IPOs & Stock Market Watch — Friday, 21 August 2026

## Headline Trends

African equities remain broadly constructive, but the advance is uneven. Latest available exchange data for 20 August show the Ghana Stock Exchange Composite at 15,065.05, down 0.69% on the day but up 71.77% year to date. BRVM Composite rose 0.75% to 519.59, with year-to-date performance at 50.28%, while Nairobi's All Share Index gained 0.46% to 244.38 and is up 30.98% year to date. NGX slipped 0.30% to 240,037.80; the JSE All Share figure was 116,051.10, up 2.41% on the latest 19 August close.

The market's central primary-issuance story is Nigeria's Dangote Refinery. Reporting during the week points to a targeted October listing and a potential raise of about US$5 billion, with approximately US$1 billion of backing reported ahead of the offer. The opportunity is substantial, but the timetable, valuation, free float and final prospectus must be treated as reported pipeline information until formally confirmed by the issuer, SEC and NGX.

## Sentiment Snapshot

Sentiment is **mixed but opportunity-seeking**. Investors are rewarding earnings, scarcity value and credible corporate actions, particularly in Ghana, BRVM and Kenya. Yet the scale of year-to-date gains on several smaller or less liquid exchanges increases the risk of sharp profit-taking, concentration and weak exit liquidity.

The IPO mood is more optimistic than the daily index tape. Recent oversubscribed offerings at Kasapreko and ZEN Petroleum in Ghana, Bridge Bank Group on BRVM and Kenya's USD-denominated green REIT demonstrate that investors will fund recognisable businesses and differentiated instruments. The challenge is converting one-off demand into a deeper secondary market.

## Deep Dive

### Africa — exchanges and movers

- **GSE:** The index closed at 15,065.05 on 20 August, with reported value traded of GHS12.96 million, volume of 4.59 million and market capitalisation of about GHS259.20 billion. Dannex Ayrton Starwin was the strongest reported GSE gainer at 1.16, up 9.43%. Intraday decliners included Enterprise Group, down 9.93%, and Hords, down 8.54%. The larger signal is structural: the GSE has produced a 71.77% year-to-date advance and welcomed First Atlantic Bank, ZEN Petroleum and Kasapreko during 2026. Kasapreko's IPO was reported as 146% oversubscribed, reinforcing demand for established consumer brands.
- **NGX:** The All-Share Index eased 0.30% to 240,037.80 on 20 August. Haldane McCall was among the leading reported gainers at 3.85, up 9.38%, while International Energy Insurance fell 9.85%, Coronation Insurance 9.84% and AVA Capital 7.89%. The market is also processing insurer recapitalisation, with press reports indicating substantial sector fundraising. The more important catalyst is Dangote Refinery: a successful local and potentially pan-African offer could materially expand NGX's market depth, but it would also test retail allocation, settlement capacity and institutional liquidity.
- **BRVM:** The regional index advanced to 519.59, with reported value traded of FCFA3.50 billion and market capitalisation of roughly FCFA20.04 trillion. TotalEnergies Marketing Senegal gained 7.16%, NEI-CEDA 7.06% and CIE 5.19%; SAPH fell 7.45%. Bridge Bank Group's Côte d'Ivoire IPO was reported fully subscribed in under 24 hours after a targeted raise of about US$118 million. That is a useful proof point for regional, francophone distribution when the issuer has a credible banking franchise.
- **Nairobi Securities Exchange:** The NSE All Share Index rose 0.46% to 244.38. Reported value traded was KES1.79 billion, volume 35.02 million and market capitalisation KES3.82 trillion. The exchange's recent product development is notable: Kenya's first USD-denominated green REIT, TRIFIC Green USD I-REIT, listed in July. This widens the investable set for dollar-based investors, including diaspora and international allocators, although liquidity and distribution remain the commercial tests.
- **JSE:** The FTSE/JSE Africa All Share Index stood at 116,051.10 on the latest 19 August close, up 2.41% on the day and 14.68% over one year. The JSE has added an actively managed global AI ETF and the Oribi South Africa Top 30 ETF, illustrating continued demand for packaged, thematic exposure rather than only single-company listings.

### Caribbean exchanges

- **Trinidad & Tobago Stock Exchange:** The TTSE Composite closed on 20 August at 1,016.67, up 1.12 points; the All T&T index rose 3.90% to 1,434.43, while the cross-listed index fell 0.27%. Republic Financial Holdings, Scotiabank Trinidad and Tobago, First Citizens and Massy were among the more actively traded names. The exchange reported 26 million units of bond activity in B048 and no mutual-fund activity. A Trinidad SME importer was reported as preparing to go public, which would be a modest but important test of the SME pipeline.
- **Jamaica Stock Exchange:** Direct exchange data were not accessible in this run, but Jamaican reporting indicates that KPREIT has put its capital raise back in motion. This is worth watching because REITs and property vehicles can provide a more investable bridge between local savings, diaspora capital and productive real estate than another thinly traded ordinary share.
- **Eastern Caribbean Securities Exchange:** No clearly verified new listing or major capital raise was identified in the current monitoring window. Liquidity and disclosure remain the limiting factors across much of the Eastern Caribbean rather than a lack of investable themes.

### IPOs and capital raises

1. **Dangote Refinery, Nigeria:** Reported target of roughly US$5 billion, potentially in October 2026, with NGX as the Nigerian anchor and possible wider African participation. This is a pipeline event, not yet a completed IPO. The commercial variables are the final valuation, free float, domestic allocation, currency exposure, cornerstone investors and whether the structure genuinely broadens ownership.
2. **Bridge Bank Group, BRVM:** Public offering launched in July and reported fully subscribed in under 24 hours. This is a recently completed subscription milestone; investors should now watch admission, first-day liquidity and post-listing price discovery.
3. **Kasapreko, GSE:** Completed a reported 146%-oversubscribed offering and began trading in June. The listing shows the GSE can attract retail demand around a well-known Ghanaian consumer brand, but sustained institutional research coverage will matter more than the launch-day enthusiasm.
4. **KPREIT, Jamaica:** Capital raise reportedly restarted in August. Treat the transaction terms, timetable and regulatory approvals as unconfirmed until the issuer publishes the formal offer documentation.
5. **Trinidad SME importer:** Reported as preparing to go public. This is early-stage pipeline information, but it could help establish a repeatable SME listing route on TTSE.

### Diaspora investment products

No newly launched, exchange-listed product explicitly dedicated to African or Caribbean diaspora equity investment was verified in this monitoring window. The stronger evidence is for enabling infrastructure: Kenya's M-Pesa-linked Ziidi Trader is bringing exchange access closer to ordinary savers, and the broader African Exchange Linkage Project is aimed at improving cross-border market access. These are not diaspora products, but they reduce the distribution friction that a credible diaspora product would need to solve.

In the Caribbean, Guyana's reported diaspora investment bond is relevant as a sovereign product, though it is not an exchange-listed equity instrument. The practical gap remains a regulated, low-ticket product that allows diaspora investors to participate in primary offers with clear FX, custody, tax and repatriation treatment.

## Commercial Opportunity

The most interesting opportunity is **primary-market distribution and investor infrastructure**, rather than trying to pick the next daily gainer. A Ghanaian, Nigerian or BRVM issuer can generate demand for an IPO, yet many potential investors still face fragmented onboarding, weak research, uncertain allocation mechanics, FX friction and limited post-listing liquidity.

A commercially sensible product would be a regulated, mobile-first capital-markets access layer for diaspora and affluent African retail investors. It should combine verified KYC, local-currency and hard-currency funding, prospectus education, IPO expression-of-interest, transparent allocation, custody, dividend collection and secondary-market execution. Ghana's MoMo rails, Nigeria's bank and fintech ecosystem, and Kenya's M-Pesa distribution provide useful local precedents; the product should not pretend that one pan-African licence removes country-by-country regulatory obligations.

The adjacent B2B opportunity is issuer readiness: financial modelling, governance, IFRS reporting, prospectus preparation, investor relations and post-listing liquidity support for mid-sized companies. The current IPO wave suggests demand exists, but the durable value sits in making companies investable and keeping them investable after the opening bell.

## Watch List

- **Dangote Refinery:** Confirm the SEC filing status, October timetable, valuation, offer size, free float, cornerstone investors and whether a JSE or other African listing is part of the final structure.
- **GSE post-IPO liquidity:** Track Kasapreko, ZEN Petroleum and First Atlantic Bank volumes, spreads and institutional ownership rather than relying on subscription statistics alone.
- **Bridge Bank on BRVM:** Watch admission date, first trading sessions, foreign participation and whether the regional exchange can sustain turnover after the IPO spotlight fades.
- **KPREIT and the TTSE SME pipeline:** Look for formal offer documents, pricing, approvals and bookbuild dates; these will indicate whether Caribbean markets can convert property and SME demand into repeatable issuance.
- **Diaspora access:** Monitor whether Ziidi Trader, AELP or a new regulated platform adds cross-border onboarding, dollar access or primary-market allocation rather than only local secondary trading.
