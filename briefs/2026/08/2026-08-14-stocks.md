# 📈 IPOs & Stock Market Watch — Friday, 14 August 2026

## Headline Trends

The latest available exchange data, dated 13 August, keeps the African picture constructive but increasingly selective. Ghana's GSE Composite stood at 15,279.24, up 0.03% on the day and 74.22% year to date, with GHS4.45 million traded and market capitalisation of roughly GHS262.82 billion. The BRVM Composite rose 0.82% to 496.22, up 43.52% year to date, on FCFA3.65 billion of value traded.

Kenya's NSE All Share gained 0.38% to 237.91 and is up 27.51% year to date. Its notable product innovation is Kenya's first US-dollar-denominated green I-REIT, TRIFIC, which broadens the exchange's appeal to hard-currency and impact-oriented capital. South Africa was softer: the FTSE/JSE All Share fell 0.83% to 114,980.90 on 12 August, although the JSE continues to add investable products, including actively managed AI and South Africa Top 30 ETFs.

Nigeria's NGX All-Share Index was quoted at 243,017.38, down 0.39% in the latest regional-market snapshot. The more important story is primary-market depth: AVA Capital gained 10% on its first NGX trading day, while Dangote Refinery has formally filed an IPO application with Nigeria's SEC according to African-markets reporting. The Dangote transaction could become Africa's defining test of domestic institutional and diaspora demand.

Caribbean trading remains more mixed. The Trinidad & Tobago Stock Exchange Composite fell 6.22 points to 1,002.39 on 13 August, while the All T&T index fell 14.47 points to 1,423.89. Individual activity was concentrated in a handful of names, including NGL, GKC, AMCL, and National Enterprises. Jamaica's exchange has shown stronger structural momentum through its new Micro Market, a reported pipeline of 25 potential companies, and electronic trading for Government of Jamaica bonds.

## Sentiment Snapshot

Sentiment is **mixed, with a bullish bias towards primary issuance and product innovation**. Equity performance in Ghana, BRVM and Kenya is strong, but the gains are concentrated and should not be mistaken for uniformly deep liquidity. Investors are rewarding credible issuers, hard-currency structures, green assets and exposure to essential consumption or infrastructure.

The caution is valuation, market breadth and exit liquidity. Jamaica's recent commentary highlights an anaemic exchange and smaller deals, whilst TTSE's index decline and thin trading in many securities show the limitations of small-market liquidity. The potential Dangote IPO is therefore commercially significant not simply because of size, but because it may bring new domestic, regional and diaspora investors into African public markets.

## Deep Dive

### 1. Ghana Stock Exchange

GSE performance remains the standout among the monitored West African exchanges. The index is up 74.22% year to date and 105.28% over one year, but the latest session itself was almost flat. That combination suggests a market that has repriced substantially and may now require earnings delivery, dividend visibility and fresh listings to sustain momentum.

Kasapreko's June listing after a reported 146% oversubscribed IPO is the clearest recent proof of retail and institutional appetite. First Atlantic Bank's listing and GSE's ongoing market-infrastructure work also point towards a broader pipeline, although no new same-day Ghana IPO was verified in the sources reviewed.

### 2. Nigerian Exchange and IPO pipeline

NGX's latest index move was negative, but the pipeline is much more compelling than the daily price action. AVA Capital's 10% first-day gain indicates demand for a well-positioned new issue. Dangote Refinery's SEC filing is the major watch item. Reports have discussed a potential multi-exchange transaction and a valuation around US$39 billion after a US$1 billion pre-IPO raise, but the final offer size, timetable, pricing and exchange structure should be treated as unconfirmed until the prospectus and regulatory notices are published.

Commercially, the transaction could create a new benchmark for local-currency participation, pension-fund allocation, book-building, research coverage and post-listing liquidity. It may also expose weaknesses in settlement, investor education and access for smaller diaspora investors if the distribution architecture is not designed carefully.

### 3. BRVM and wider West Africa

BRVM is combining strong index performance with genuine primary-market activity. Bridge Bank Group Côte d'Ivoire's reported US$118 million IPO was fully subscribed in under 24 hours, a powerful signal for regional banking and consumer-facing issuers with credible growth stories. The exchange's 43.52% year-to-date rise supports the case for more listings, but the next test is secondary-market turnover after the initial allocation excitement.

The most attractive BRVM opportunities are likely to sit in issuer preparation, bilingual investor relations, cross-border brokerage access and regional research rather than in simply launching another retail trading app.

### 4. Nairobi and Johannesburg

NSE is up 27.51% year to date and is showing useful product breadth. TRIFIC's USD green I-REIT offers a model for mobilising foreign and diaspora capital while reducing direct local-currency exposure. NSE has also launched options on its NEXT derivatives market, which, if liquidity develops, can improve hedging and price discovery.

JSE's latest index reading was weaker, but product development remains active. New AI-focused and South Africa Top 30 ETFs show that the exchange can package thematic exposure for modern investors even in a mature market. This is relevant to African exchanges seeking to broaden participation beyond conventional single-stock investing.

### 5. Caribbean exchanges

TTSE's 13 August summary showed a down day, with activity concentrated in a limited group of liquid shares. The exchange's cross-border depository links with Jamaica, Barbados and the Eastern Caribbean are strategically useful, but the commercial constraint remains low turnover and limited issuer supply.

Jamaica is taking a more promising market-development approach. The Micro Market is intended to give smaller companies a realistic route towards public capital, while electronic Government-bond trading can improve access to fixed income. Quantas Advantage's July main-market listing demonstrates that new issuance is possible, but the exchange still needs a consistent pipeline, better disclosure discipline and stronger market-making.

### 6. Diaspora investment products

No newly launched, exchange-specific African or Caribbean diaspora product was independently verified for 14 August. The direction of travel is nevertheless clear: Daba is marketing access to African markets from Canada, and UBA has been repositioning diaspora banking around remittances and investment. These are reported products and positioning initiatives rather than proof of a new regulated fund or exchange-traded diaspora vehicle.

The gap is commercially attractive. A compliant platform that combines diaspora onboarding, KYC, FX conversion, fractional or pooled access to IPOs, custody, tax documentation and plain-English research would address a real distribution problem. It should begin with regulated brokerage and partner-led execution rather than attempting to become an exchange.

## Commercial Opportunity

The strongest opportunity is **primary-market distribution infrastructure for African and Caribbean issuers**. The market does not primarily need another generic trading app; it needs trusted rails that convert diaspora savings and regional retail demand into properly governed, documented and settled investment.

A sensible first product would be a Ghana- or Nigeria-focused capital-markets service with three layers: issuer readiness and investor-relations support; a regulated partner for execution and custody; and a diaspora-facing subscription for verified IPO alerts, prospectus education, allocation workflows and post-listing research. BRVM and Jamaica can be added through local partners once the compliance and settlement model works in one market.

The immediate wedge is the next large Nigerian or Ghanaian offering. Revenue can come from issuer mandates, research subscriptions, distribution fees where permitted, FX and custody partnerships, and recurring SME listing-readiness work. The principal risks are securities licensing, cross-border solicitation, FX controls, allocation fairness, data protection and the danger of promising liquidity where the underlying market is thin.

## Watch List

- **Dangote Refinery IPO:** confirm SEC clearance, prospectus, valuation, offer size, local versus international tranches, and whether multiple exchange listings proceed.
- **Bridge Bank Group Côte d'Ivoire:** monitor listing date, free float, opening price, turnover and whether the rapid subscription converts into durable BRVM liquidity.
- **GSE listing pipeline:** watch for further bank, consumer and industrial listings following Kasapreko and First Atlantic Bank.
- **Jamaica Micro Market:** track the 25-company pipeline, disclosure quality, intermediary support and whether the segment creates repeatable SME issuance.
- **TTSE liquidity:** monitor whether index weakness is accompanied by improving turnover in the most actively traded names or simply wider illiquidity.
- **Diaspora rails:** look for regulated products that move from general diaspora banking and remittance messaging into actual custody, brokerage and IPO allocation.
