# 📦 Import & Export Trade Trends — Sunday, 15 November 2026 — EXPORT Week

## Headline Trends
ISO week 46 is even: this is an export-focused edition. The most useful current signal is a Ghana Business News report, surfaced on 11 November, that government has launched REWARD-Ghana to strengthen rice production. It is a policy and production signal—not evidence of exportable surplus yet. On the import side, recent reporting continues to flag Ghana's fuel and vehicle bill, while the cost of FX, freight and customs clearance remains central to landed prices.

## Sentiment Snapshot
Mixed. There is a clear regional ambition to replace imported food and move beyond raw commodity exports, but headlines and programme launches should not be mistaken for audited trade flows or commercial offtake. Exporters face compliance, quality, aggregation and logistics constraints; importers face currency exposure and uncertain clearance times. Current research did not establish comparable same-day physical import/export volumes for Ghana, Nigeria and Côte d’Ivoire, so this brief avoids presenting stale or unverified numbers as current totals.

## Deep Dive
### 1. Top West African Exports — Current Volumes & Trends

A useful discipline is to separate official physical volumes from targets and market commentary. The sources available for this run do not provide a harmonised, current customs series for each requested commodity. The following is a trade map of the material flows, with the evidence limitation explicit:

- **Cocoa:** Côte d’Ivoire and Ghana are the region's anchor origins. Ghana's FAO country brief has previously characterised output as having fallen over multiple consecutive years; the latest crop and shipment totals should be checked against COCOBOD/Conseil du Café-Cacao and customs releases before pricing a contract. Bean exports still dominate value capture; processing margins depend on reliable beans, energy, quality and confirmed buyers.
- **Gold:** Ghana, Burkina Faso and Mali remain key gold-exporting jurisdictions. Gold's high value-to-weight ratio makes it less exposed to container freight, but traceability, licensing, responsible sourcing and FX repatriation are decisive. No fresh country shipment tonnage was verified for this edition.
- **Cashew and shea:** Ghana, Côte d’Ivoire, Benin, Burkina Faso and Nigeria supply raw nuts and kernels, shea kernels and butter. The commercial opportunity is in graded, moisture-controlled, traceable lots and local processing; no current season-to-date audited volume was verified here.
- **Bauxite:** Guinea is the region's major bauxite exporter. Guinea's quarterly aggregate exports are not a bauxite-specific measure, and should not be represented as such. For commercial analysis, use mine/port disclosures and vessel/customs data; aluminium prices are only a directional proxy, not an ore quotation.
- **Crude oil:** Nigeria, Ghana and Côte d’Ivoire export crude. Production, liftings and export receipts differ; do not infer shipment volume from output or a budget target. No comparable latest cargo series was confirmed for this brief.
- **Rubber and timber:** Côte d’Ivoire is a major natural-rubber origin; Ghana and Côte d’Ivoire also export timber and wood products. EU deforestation rules increase the value of plot-level traceability, legality records and compliant chain-of-custody. Timber and rubber market access is increasingly a data-and-compliance problem as much as a production problem.

**New export openings:** regional buyers for processed foods, rice, shea butter, cashew kernels, cocoa liquor/butter and compliant wood products; and intra-African markets where AfCFTA preferences can apply. The test is not the headline tariff alone: confirm rules of origin, standards, border procedures, payment security and distributor capacity. For rice in particular, the November REWARD-Ghana announcement supports a domestic production agenda; exportability is conditional on yields, milling quality and domestic food availability.

**Value addition:** movement is real in policy intent but uneven in execution. Modular processing—drying, cleaning, grading, milling, cold storage and traceability—can capture value with lower fixed-capital risk than a large plant. Secure feedstock and an offtake specification first; validate electricity, water, packaging, working capital and export documentation before buying machinery.

### 2. Imports — Short Regional Read

Recent Ghana coverage identifies fuel and vehicles among major import-bill components, and the broader import basket includes rice, wheat, pharmaceuticals, machinery, electronics and industrial inputs. Nigeria's import profile is similarly exposed to machinery, refined fuels/energy products, vehicles and food, although domestic refining changes the composition rather than removing the exposure. Côte d’Ivoire imports cereals, machinery, fuel and pharmaceuticals; a fresh category-level figure was not independently verified for this edition. Product rankings are structural context, not a claim of current month-by-month quantities.

Import-substitution candidates are rice and selected processed foods, generic medicines where quality-assured local production is viable, packaging, animal feed and repair/maintenance parts. These require a landed-cost comparison—not just a tariff comparison—including power, finance, quality certification, raw materials and scale. Substitution fails commercially where local unit cost or reliability is materially worse than imports.

### 3. Export Infrastructure, Import Costs & Logistics

Tema and Takoradi are Ghana's principal gateways; Abidjan handles major Côte d’Ivoire flows; Lagos and its newer Lekki gateway serve Nigeria. This research run did not produce live, corridor-specific carrier quotations, port dwell-time readings or a current congestion index, so no generic container price is offered. Obtain quotes by origin/destination, equipment, sailing, surcharge, free time and validity date; quote inland haulage separately.

The practical cost stack is ocean freight + insurance + terminal and customs charges + demurrage/detention + inland haulage + FX conversion and financing. Ghana's US Trade Administration customs guide reports that roughly 60–80% of imports remain subject to arrival inspection and describes lengthy, erratic clearance, port-road congestion and the lack of functioning freight rail as sources of delay and loss. Perishables and time-sensitive inputs are particularly exposed. Buyers should model buffer stock and working capital against actual broker and carrier performance, not an advertised port tariff alone.

### 4. Trade Policy

AfCFTA offers preferential access in principle, subject to product-specific rules of origin and national implementation. For Ghana-EU and Côte d’Ivoire-EU supply chains, deforestation-free and traceability requirements matter for cocoa, rubber and timber. The latest news discovery also surfaced the REWARD-Ghana rice initiative; details on funding, procurement and implementation need confirmation from official programme documents. No operative new regional tariff, import ban or preferential change was verified in this run, so none is presented as enacted policy.

## Commercial Opportunity
**Best near-term, lower-capital angle: a buyer-backed Ghana rice aggregation and quality-assurance pilot—not speculative export farming.** Partner with existing growers/millers in a producing district; aggregate a defined batch, measure moisture and foreign matter, arrange drying/milling and deliver to a named institutional buyer, wholesaler or retailer. Confirm REWARD-Ghana eligibility and local supply before making claims that the programme guarantees finance or purchases. Track three pilot metrics: accepted tonnes, rejection rate and gross margin per delivered tonne. Only scale towards regional sales once domestic demand is met, quality is consistent and the AfCFTA origin and border requirements are documented.

A second, service-led opportunity is clearance and delivery coordination for importers: pre-check documents, track milestones and arrange inland haulage. Sell a measurable reduction in avoidable storage/demurrage and stock-out risk; do not promise customs outcomes outside the operator's control.

## Watch List
- **Ghana rice programme:** official implementation, eligible districts, financing, input access and actual offtake—not just launch headlines.
- **FX and settlement:** cedi, naira and CFA movement against supplier invoice currencies; agree payment windows and hedge or price-reset clauses where feasible.
- **Freight and port service:** obtain fresh quotes and track vessel arrival-to-release, free time and inland truck turn-time by gateway.
- **Traceability rules:** buyer deadlines and documentation requirements for cocoa, rubber and timber entering regulated markets.
- **Verified trade data:** next releases from GSS, NBS Nigeria, customs agencies and national commodity bodies; reconcile tonnes and values before underwriting shipments.
