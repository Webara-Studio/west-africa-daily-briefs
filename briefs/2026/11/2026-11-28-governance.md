# 🌍 Governance, Elections, Regulation & Trade — Saturday, 28 November 2026

## Headline Trends
Ghana’s constitutional reform consultations have surfaced public disagreement over proposals to lengthen presidential and parliamentary terms and the design of local executive elections; these remain proposals, not enacted changes. Ghana also announced a planned three-month seasonal restriction on raw cashew exports, prompting producer opposition, while a new Ghana–Côte d’Ivoire mobile-money remittance link and Ghana’s timber legality credentials offer concrete regional trade developments. ECOWAS has funded election-related support in The Gambia ahead of its 5 December presidential vote and is urging stronger collective responses to regional security threats.

## Sentiment Snapshot
**Mixed, with a practical opportunity bias.** Regulatory uncertainty around cashew exports, constitutional reform and regional security calls for monitoring rather than treating announcements as settled rules. Conversely, operationally launched digital remittance rails and verified timber licensing provide identifiable routes to market. Regional monetary integration is again being discussed, but ECOWAS’s 2027 Eco ambition is a policy programme, not a near-term currency or settlement change for businesses.

## Deep Dive

### 1. Elections & Political Developments
ECOWAS says it has provided **US$480,000** to The Gambia’s Independent Electoral Commission and six other public and civil-society institutions to support the presidential election scheduled for **5 December 2026**. This is concrete election-preparation support; the official announcement does not establish the eventual election outcome or guarantee a smooth post-election period. Businesses with staff, inventory or deliveries in The Gambia should review contingency contacts, travel plans and election-week operating arrangements.

In Ghana, civil-society groups engaged by the Constitutional Reform Implementation Committee have opposed the proposed extension of presidential and parliamentary terms from four to five years and called for citizens to elect local government leaders without presidential interference. The committee reported agreement on approximately 89% of the government’s reform proposals, but the disputed items remain part of a consultation process. The commercial implication is to monitor the eventual draft legislation and parliamentary process rather than price in a constitutional change prematurely.

Nigeria’s political coverage is already turning towards the 2027 cycle, but the material reviewed for this brief does not establish a new national election timetable or change to the rules. Avoid reading campaign activity as a regulatory development.

### 2. Regulatory Changes
Ghana’s proposed three-month seasonal ban on exports of raw cashew is the clearest immediate policy risk. The announcement was made in connection with the inauguration of a processing factory, with the stated aim of supplying domestic processors. The Ghana Cooperative Cashew Farmers and Marketing Association objects that processors may not have capacity to absorb and store the crop, potentially increasing post-harvest losses and reducing farm incomes. The report describes an anticipated measure; this review did not locate an operative legal instrument, commencement date, enforcement mechanism or exemption list. Exporters, processors and lenders should therefore scenario-plan, but should not treat the restriction as already in force.

Ghana’s constitutional proposals are likewise not enacted law. No verified new same-day tax, central-bank, securities or data-protection directive affecting the countries in scope was identified in the sources reviewed; this is a monitoring finding, not proof that no such notice exists.

### 3. Trade Deals & Agreements
Mobile Money Fintech Limited and Ecobank Ghana have launched Ecobank Rapid Transfer, enabling MTN MoMo users in Côte d’Ivoire to send funds directly to Ghanaian MoMo wallets. The report says the service received Bank of Ghana approval and may expand to other African markets subject to the required regulatory processes. It is a functioning bilateral payment corridor, not a general waiver of FX, AML/KYC, consumer-protection or transaction-reporting obligations. Merchants and transfer businesses should test total fees, FX conversion, settlement time, limits and dispute handling before relying on it for working capital.

Ghana’s Lands Minister reported more than 103,000 FLEGT licences covering over 48,000 cubic metres of wood products destined for 19 EU member states, and called for more processed products to be included in the agreement’s scope. The same report notes EU Deforestation Regulation requirements are scheduled to apply at the end of December 2026. FLEGT is a valuable legality and market-access credential; it should not be assumed to satisfy every separate buyer or EUDR due-diligence requirement. Exporters need product-level traceability and confirmation of the applicable compliance process with EU buyers.

ECOWAS’s ministerial committee called for decisive action towards a phased Eco single-currency launch from 2027. This is a stated regional ambition, not evidence of an adopted conversion date, harmonised monetary conditions or available business settlement product. The Commission has also consulted national transport ministries and road agencies on regional road-safety, axle-load, maintenance and transport-information governance. Those consultations may eventually improve corridor predictability, but are not themselves a new enforceable regional rule.

ECOWAS and the African Development Bank announced the launch of the Regional Resilient Rice Value Chains Development Programme (REWARD-ECOWAS). This points to public and development-finance attention on regional rice supply chains; the announcement alone does not specify a commercial procurement opportunity or funding terms for individual firms.

### 4. Mining & Extractives
The most material current extractives signal in the sources reviewed is Ghana’s Damang mine governance scrutiny. CHRAJ said its investigation found no evidence of wrongdoing by the President, Vice-President or Engineers & Planners in the mine acquisition or use of a private aircraft. The commission reported that the President recused himself from the relevant Cabinet meeting and that the acquisition process had begun under the previous administration. This is the regulator’s reported finding on a complaint, not a general assurance on licensing or future mine approvals. Investors should retain auditable records of tendering, beneficial ownership, recusal and government engagement.

Separately, Ghana Business News reported that 13 people accused of impersonating anti-illegal-mining taskforce officers and extorting miners were remanded by a court. The allegations underline a practical site-level risk: verify officials’ identities and written authority, keep incident logs, and escalate demands through established legal and security channels. No verified new licence award, discovery or change in mining law was identified in the current source review.

### 5. Security & Stability
ECOWAS has renewed its call for coordinated regional responses to increasingly complex political, security and humanitarian challenges. Its public statement is a strategic warning rather than a specific new security incident or border closure. For operators, the persistent concern is uneven security and route reliability across parts of the Sahel and border corridors, which can affect insurance, staff movement, lead times and inventory buffers. The Gambia’s approaching election adds a near-term need for local operating contingencies. The reviewed sources did not verify a fresh cross-border closure or a new region-wide security measure today.

## Commercial Opportunity
**Largest near-term opportunity: compliant payment and trade enablement across the Ghana–Côte d’Ivoire corridor.** The MoMo–Ecobank launch gives SMEs and service providers a live channel to test lower-friction person-to-person and business-linked transfers. Start with a controlled pilot involving a defined merchant cohort; measure end-to-end cost, FX transparency, settlement time, failed-payment rate and reconciliation effort before scaling. Do not assume consumer remittance approval automatically authorises every business collection or payout use case.

**Largest immediate regulatory risk: the proposed Ghana cashew export restriction.** A sudden seasonal control could strand stock, weaken farmgate prices or disrupt exporter commitments if domestic processing capacity is inadequate. Exporters should obtain written clarification from the responsible authority, map inventory by harvest period, review force-majeure and delivery clauses, and model a domestic-processing fallback. Processors can explore capacity partnerships and forward supply contracts, but only against verified throughput, storage, working-capital and quality specifications.

A second investable niche is timber export compliance: help small and medium-sized mills connect FLEGT documentation with buyer-required plot-level traceability, evidence management and processed-product specifications. The commercial test is buyer-confirmed acceptance and compliance cost per shipment, not the headline licence count alone.

## Watch List
- **The Gambia, 5 December 2026:** follow the electoral commission and ECOWAS updates; assess election-day access, staffing and delivery contingencies.
- **Ghana cashew measure:** seek the actual legal notice, dates, scope, enforcement plan and any exceptions before shipping or buying decisions.
- **Ghana constitutional reform:** track draft amendments, consultations and parliamentary action; current term-length and local-executive proposals are not law.
- **EU timber compliance at year-end:** confirm with buyers and competent authorities which FLEGT documents support, but do not replace, applicable deforestation due diligence.
- **ECOWAS transport and Eco programmes:** distinguish consultations and political targets from binding regulations and operational settlement systems.
