## Commercial Opportunity

The most actionable near-term angle is **payments operations and compliance infrastructure**, not launching a speculative token or an unlicensed consumer exchange. Ghanaian and Nigerian importers, exporters and service firms need predictable cross-border settlement, transparent fees, reconciliation and reliable local payout. A commercially sensible pilot would:

1. Interview a narrow cohort of businesses with recurring UK–Ghana or US–Nigeria payments and document their current cost, timing, failure and reconciliation pain.
2. Compare bank, remittance-provider and stablecoin-enabled routes on total delivered local currency, including conversion spread, network fee, payout charge, FX slippage and failed-payment handling.
3. Use an appropriately authorised VASP/payment partner for any regulated conversion, transfer or custody; obtain local legal advice and written partner scope before processing customer funds.
4. Pilot with a small number of business payments, maintain transaction-level audit and complaints records, and prove net savings and payout reliability before scaling.

The principal risk is building around a presumed licence exemption or a headline transfer fee while overlooking local payout liquidity, compliance obligations, FX volatility, consumer recourse and counterparty concentration. Avoid customer custody and public yield/profit promises in the initial test.
