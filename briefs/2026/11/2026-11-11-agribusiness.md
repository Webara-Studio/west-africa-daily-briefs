# 🌾 Agribusiness & Commodities — Wednesday, 11 November 2026

## Headline Trends
Global cocoa’s latest available close fell to **US$4,724/tonne on 10 November**, down 5.6% day-on-day and 16.8% over the month, according to Trading Economics. Ghana policy news points to a relaunch of free cocoa fertiliser with digital monitoring; in Côte d’Ivoire, reports flag poor rainfall as a risk to the coming mid-crop. The most investable theme is turning harvest into reliable, quality-assured supply through aggregation, traceability and post-harvest services.

## Sentiment Snapshot
**Mixed, with a cautious cocoa bias.** The benchmark correction and rainfall concern complicate near-term grower and buyer planning, while cheaper prices may improve processors’ procurement economics if quality and supply hold. Ghana’s fertiliser announcement is supportive in principle, but an allocation or launch is not proof of timely delivery or yield response. Gold and crude remain high in absolute terms, but short-term direction is mixed. No new official production or export-volume release was verified in this review.

### 2. Production, harvest and exports
A 10 November Google News discovery result reported concerns that poor rains could nearly halve Côte d’Ivoire’s mid-crop forecast; another report framed potential cocoa production losses of up to a quarter under El Niño conditions. These are **reported risk estimates**, not a verified official harvest forecast or realised output. No new authoritative Ghana, Nigeria, Côte d’Ivoire or Senegal crop-yield/export-volume release was confirmed in the reviewed sources. Historical figures should not be passed off as new data.

A dated CNBC Africa report (6 November) says Côte d’Ivoire rubber production reached **2 million tonnes in 2025**, with farmers shifting from cocoa towards rubber. This is a reported 2025 figure, not a newly released 2026 production total; verify against the national rubber regulator before investment or feedstock commitments.

### 3. Investment and commercial activity
The most concrete current signal identified is operational rather than a factory financing round: Ghana’s COCOBOD is reported to have relaunched free fertiliser distribution with digital tracking. Separately, a 7 November report describes IFC and Senegalese agri-tech Tolbi targeting 2 million farmers and cooperatives. Treat the latter as a reported target/partnership, not proof that 2 million farmers are already served or that investment capital has been disbursed. No independently verified new cold-chain, irrigation or processing-facility financial close was identified in this review.

### 4. Policy and subsidies
MyJoyOnline reported on 6 November that COCOBOD relaunched a free fertiliser programme with a monitoring platform; GhanaWeb separately reported a **3.5 million-bag** allocation. The figure and programme terms need confirmation against a COCOBOD circular. For operators, the important tests are district-level delivery, input timing, beneficiary verification and whether distribution reaches farms before the relevant application window. No confirmed new export restriction or agricultural-zone policy was found in the reviewed current window.

### 5. Climate and weather
Near-term crop risk is most visible in the reported Côte d’Ivoire rainfall concerns for the mid-crop. Treat the forecast as a warning to monitor, not proof of crop failure. GMet’s seasonal forecast catalogue is the primary Ghana reference; this review did not verify a newly issued November-specific bulletin for Ghana, Nigeria, Senegal or Côte d’Ivoire. Practical exposures include irregular pod development, harvest timing, drying quality, feeder-road access and the cost of maintaining consistent supply. Obtain current district-level advisories before making crop or insurance decisions.

## Commercial Opportunity
**Best angle now: buyer-backed, traceable cocoa aggregation with moisture control and reliable drying, piloted in one producing district in Ghana or Côte d’Ivoire.** Falling benchmark prices make inventory speculation particularly unattractive; operational services can earn through quality premiums, lower rejection and dependable delivery instead. Secure a licensed-buyer/offtaker agreement first, define accepted moisture and traceability records, then run a one-season pilot. Measure kilograms accepted at first inspection, rejection rate, days from collection to buyer payment and farmer repeat participation. Keep working-capital exposure capped; validate unit economics before adding a dryer, warehouse or cold-chain asset.

A second, conditional opportunity is digital farm-service distribution in Senegal: partner with an established cooperative or agritech provider and test agronomy, input access and collection logistics against verified farmer use—not headline reach targets.
