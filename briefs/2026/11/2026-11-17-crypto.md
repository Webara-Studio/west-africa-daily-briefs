## Commercial Opportunity

The strongest practical angle is **not launching another speculative token or unlicensed retail exchange**. It is a tightly scoped, compliance-first settlement pilot for a real business-payment corridor—potentially UK→Ghana—using appropriately authorised partners and a clear local-currency payout route, including MoMo where permitted.

Start with a narrow use case (for example, recurring supplier invoices), and compare the complete cost and service level with bank and remittance alternatives. Measure all-in cost per transfer, settlement time, payout success, FX slippage, reconciliation effort and compliance exceptions. Secure legal review and partner sign-off before handling customer funds; keep custody and conversion with eligible providers. If BoG's reported directive is issued, adapt the pilot against its actual text rather than its headlines.

A second, lower-risk opportunity is compliance and treasury tooling for banks, remittance firms and payment providers: counterparty screening, wallet exposure checks, transaction monitoring, audit trails and reconciliation across fiat, MoMo and digital-asset legs.

## Watch List

- **BoG stablecoin directive:** Watch for the actual text, publication date, scope, transition period and treatment of issuers, intermediaries, custody and payments.
- **Licensing registers and notices:** Confirm current Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya and South Africa requirements directly with regulators before selecting partners.
- **e-Cedi:** Seek a dated official update on pilot status and interoperability; do not assume CBDC progress authorises private stablecoin services.
- **Corridor economics:** Look for independently measured UK–Ghana and US–Nigeria all-in costs and payout performance, not headline blockchain volume.
- **Exchange and Web3 disclosures:** Treat company announcements as claims until corroborated; distinguish product launches, sandbox participation and full activity-specific authorisation.
