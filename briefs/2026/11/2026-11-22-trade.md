# 📦 Import & Export Trade Trends — Sunday, 22 November 2026 — IMPORT Week

## Headline Trends
ISO week 47 is odd, so this edition goes deeper on imports. The latest accessible Trading Economics country-series snapshots returned Ghana imports of USD 1.813bn in April 2026 (up from USD 1.542bn in March) and Nigeria imports of NGN 4.853tn in June (down from NGN 5.242tn in May). These are dated monthly macro readings, not current cargo manifests; the reference windows differ and Nigeria's nominal naira figure is not directly comparable with Ghana's dollar figure.

Recent Ghana coverage points to pressure to reduce port delays, while a 10 November CitiNewsroom report framed Ghana's food import bill at about USD 3bn. Treat that as a reported estimate, not an audited current-year total. On the supply side, FAO's latest accessible Côte d’Ivoire country brief (reference date 16 November 2026) forecasts 2026 cereal production at about 4.4m tonnes, around 20% above its five-year average, but says local rice prices in September were near or up to 10% below year-earlier levels. This is useful context, not proof of a specific import displacement.

## Sentiment Snapshot
Mixed. Falling monthly import values in Nigeria and improving Ivorian cereal prospects may ease selected food-supply pressures, but the underlying trade picture remains exposed to exchange-rate conversion, fuel and freight costs, and clearance friction. Ghana's reported food-import bill underscores demand for locally competitive staples; it does not by itself establish that local supply can meet required grade, volume, seasonality or delivered price. Recent news items are discovery evidence; policy claims require confirmation against operative customs notices.

## Deep Dive
### 1. Top West African Imports — Ghana, Nigeria and Côte d’Ivoire

**Ghana.** Trading Economics' latest available import observation in the retrieved series is USD 1,812.90m for April 2026, against USD 1,541.90m in March. The page does not provide a same-day breakdown in the accessible summary. Rice and wheat, refined fuels, machinery, electronics and pharmaceuticals remain commercially important import categories, but this brief cannot verify current category-level volumes or November arrivals. Ghana's cereal production was estimated by FAO at 6.2m tonnes in 2024, 27% above its previous five-year average; that historical aggregate includes cereals and should not be mistaken for rice self-sufficiency. Ghana's 2025 country brief is dated 19 August 2025, so its food-security and price observations are not current-market quotes.

**Nigeria.** The latest accessible macro series gives NGN 4,852,803.77m for June 2026, down from NGN 5,241,876.42m in May. In nominal naira, this reflects both quantities and prices/exchange conversion; it does not establish that physical imports fell. Fuel and agricultural input costs are a material operating exposure: FAO's Nigeria brief, dated 16 September 2026, reports domestic petrol-price increases in the first half of 2026 and fertilizer price rises between February and May (NPK +12%, urea +43%). Rice, wheat, machinery, electronics and pharmaceuticals remain plausible high-value import lanes, but no comparable current tonnage or customs breakdown was verifiable in this run.

**Côte d’Ivoire.** A current, comparable import-value figure was not exposed by the country-series endpoint checked. Do not infer one from Ghana or Nigeria. FAO's 16 November brief forecasts 4.4m tonnes of aggregate cereals in 2026 and reports September local-rice prices near or below year-earlier levels. Imports of rice, wheat, fuel, equipment, electronics and medicines still warrant buyer-by-buyer analysis; domestic production prospects do not eliminate imports of specific grades, processed foods or industrial inputs.

**Product lens.** Rice and wheat are the clearest food-security and substitution priorities. Refined petroleum products and fuels transmit international price and currency shocks into transport and production costs. Machinery and electronics are capital and productivity inputs, where repairability, spare parts and financing often matter as much as the invoice price. Pharmaceuticals require registration, quality assurance and dependable cold chain where applicable; local assembly or packaging is not a shortcut around regulatory approvals. Product-level quantities, origins and prices need validation from national customs/statistical releases before committing capital.

### 2. Import Substitution Opportunities

The strongest near-term candidate is not a blanket “replace imports” proposition; it is a narrow rice value-chain pilot in Ghana. Aggregate local cereal output is encouraging, but rice quality, drying, milling recovery, broken-grain share, packaging and dependable monthly delivery determine whether a buyer switches. Secure a miller and two anchor buyers (for example, a caterer or institutional kitchen and a regional wholesaler); aggregate from a defined production basin; test moisture, grade and delivered cost against imported equivalents; then contract only the volume that clears the buyer's specification. Côte d’Ivoire's positive 2026 cereal outlook makes it a potential source of competitive regional supply, subject to actual procurement terms and border compliance.

Other credible substitution lanes are poultry/feed inputs where local maize and soy supply can meet formulation specs; local manufacture or assembly of selected packaging, furniture and construction inputs; and pharmaceutical packaging or selected generic production where regulatory, API-sourcing and quality economics are proven. Each requires a product-specific bill of materials and landed-cost test. Import substitution fails commercially when it relies on protection alone, ignores utility and finance costs, or assumes a harvest forecast equals a year-round factory feedstock.

### 3. Import Costs & Logistics

FX is the first-order uncertainty. Ghana's import series is reported in USD, while Nigeria's is in NGN; neither provides an exchange-rate-adjusted volume index in its page summary. An importer should cost each order at the actual bank/dealer rate, include FX spread and settlement time, and stress-test a 5–10% adverse move before accepting a fixed-cedi or fixed-naira sales price. These are scenario sensitivities, not a forecast.

Port and inland charges are corridor-specific. Ghana news on 19 November reported a presidential call to clear port delays, but no measured dwell-time improvement or official fee change was verified here. For Tema, Abidjan, Apapa/Tin Can and Lekki, obtain carrier, terminal and customs quotes by commodity, container type, route and shipment date; record gate-to-gate time, inspection hold, demurrage/free days, truck turn-time and inland haulage separately. Do not apply a generic regional freight price. In Nigeria, the newer Lekki port is an alternative to evaluate, not a guarantee of faster or cheaper end-to-end delivery. In Ghana and Côte d’Ivoire, inland distribution cost and seasonal road conditions can erase an ocean-freight saving.

### 4. Trade Policy & Preferential Arrangements

AfCFTA and ECOWAS preferences can improve the economics of qualifying regional goods, but tariff preference depends on rules of origin, product classification, proof of origin and the relevant national implementation. Confirm the current tariff line and customs treatment before shipment; a trade-agreement headline is not a customs waiver. The News Agency of Nigeria reported on 11 November that Nigeria could access China's zero-tariff offer before 2028; it concerns Nigerian exports entering China, not a new import preference for goods entering Nigeria. No new operative Ghanaian, Nigerian or Ivorian import ban or tariff change was independently confirmed in this run. The World Bank's June 2026 article on Nigeria's AfCFTA trade ambitions is strategic context rather than evidence of a recently implemented import rule.

## Commercial Opportunity
Run a 60–90 day Ghana rice procurement-and-milling pilot, with a regional sourcing option from Côte d’Ivoire only after quality and border checks. The buyer is a wholesaler, caterer or institutional kitchen seeking reliable local supply; the product is graded, consistently milled and traceable rice delivered in buyer-sized packs. Begin with buyer specifications and a signed trial order, not a speculative mill investment. Measure (1) landed cost per accepted kilogram versus the buyer's imported benchmark, (2) rejection/breakage rate, (3) on-time fill rate, and (4) cash-conversion days. Proceed to larger volumes only if the pilot achieves price parity after finance, packaging, transport and quality-control costs. A parallel, lower-capital service opportunity is shipment-level landed-cost and delay tracking for importers at Tema and Lagos, billed per consignment or as a share of verified savings.

## Watch List
- **Ghana port performance:** seek published Tema clearance, dwell and fee data after the 19 November ministerial direction; the commercial benefit is real only if measurable delay falls.
- **Nigerian import statistics and FX:** next monthly data should be checked in both naira value and physical volume; nominal movements alone can mislead procurement decisions.
- **Rice harvest and market prices:** monitor Ghanaian and Ivorian harvest arrivals, moisture/grade, miller capacity and wholesale prices; these validate whether substitution is actually economic.
- **Customs implementation:** verify any tariff schedule or AfCFTA/ECOWAS origin treatment with a current official customs notice before quoting duty savings.
