# 📦 West African Import & Export Trade Trends — Sunday, 8 November 2026 — IMPORT Week

## Headline Trends
ISO week 45 is odd, so this brief focuses on imports. Recent reporting points to a substantial Ghanaian food-import bill, a sharp rise in Côte d’Ivoire’s potato imports, and persistent concerns about port delays on Ghana-bound cargo. These are signals of demand and friction, not a complete customs dataset: comparable, current commodity-by-commodity import volumes for Ghana, Nigeria and Côte d’Ivoire were not available in the material verified for this brief.

## Sentiment Snapshot
The commercial picture is mixed. Importers remain exposed to FX, freight and clearance costs, while food-import dependence creates a market for reliable local substitutes. A September report citing Ghana’s Importers and Exporters Association warned cargo delays risk diverting trade to Abidjan; this is an industry warning, not a measured market-share shift. No reliable same-day corridor freight quote or current port dwell-time series was verified, so neither is quantified here.

## Deep Dive
### 1. Top West African Imports — What is flowing in
**Ghana:** A Citi Newsroom report published 31 July 2026 said Ghana spent GH¢36bn importing chicken, rice and sugar in 2025. That is a reported combined expenditure, not a volume series or a breakdown by product. Rice remains a prominent policy focus: in June, Ghana’s agriculture minister was reported as proposing to tie rice import permits to local production. Treat this as a reported policy direction; verify an operative notice and permit conditions with the relevant authorities before making sourcing decisions.

**Nigeria:** The named priority categories for this brief are rice, wheat, fuel, machinery, electronics and pharmaceuticals. The current research did not establish recent, comparable 2026 quantities or values for those categories. The latest relevant discovery in the monitoring set was an older March report about intermodal transport constraints; it supports a logistics-risk observation, not a claim about current import totals. Importers should model landed cost using current FX access, tariff classification, port charges and inland transport quotes rather than relying on stale headline figures.

**Côte d’Ivoire:** FreshPlaza reported on 28 October that potato imports rose 45% in the first nine months of 2026. This is a reported percentage change; the accessible discovery record did not provide the baseline tonnage or value. It is a useful demand lead, not enough by itself to size a production project. Wheat, fuel, machinery, electronics and pharmaceuticals are also relevant import categories, but no current comparable category totals were verified for this brief.

### 2. Import Substitution Opportunities
Food is the most tangible opening, but the GH¢36bn figure must not be mistaken for a domestic-producible market of the same size. For rice, poultry, sugar and potatoes, test the addressable import-replacement slice product by product: local supply seasonality, quality consistency, processing, storage, working capital and the buyer’s delivered price. In Ghana, start with an institutional or wholesale buyer and a defined offtake specification; in Côte d’Ivoire, validate the potato import increase with traders and processors before expanding acreage or cold storage.

For machinery, electronics and pharmaceuticals, local assembly, repair, packaging or distribution may be more realistic first steps than full manufacturing. These depend on reliable component supply, standards compliance and after-sales capability. Any substitution proposal should compare landed import cost against local cost at the same quality and delivery point, including FX and finance costs.

### 3. Import Costs & Logistics
The import bill is sensitive to currency conversion and access to trade finance: a weaker or less available settlement currency increases local-currency working-capital requirements even where the foreign-currency invoice is unchanged. This brief does not quote spot exchange rates, freight tariffs or shipping costs because no timestamped, verifiable corridor quote was obtained.

Port delay concerns warrant a practical response rather than a generic regional freight estimate. Request quotes by origin, container type, carrier, validity window and destination port; separately price terminal handling, demurrage, customs/inspection, haulage and insurance. Compare Tema and Abidjan only for genuinely substitutable routes, accounting for inland distance, border procedures, transit risk and delivery lead time. The frequently repeated claim of severe Lagos dwell time was not independently refreshed here and should not be treated as a current benchmark.

### 4. Trade Policy & Exports in Brief
The rice-permit/local-production proposal is the clearest recent import-policy signal located, but it should not be represented as an enacted restriction without the operative directive. No new import ban or tariff change for Ghana, Nigeria or Côte d’Ivoire was verified in this run. The AfCFTA and ECOWAS frameworks can support regional sourcing, but actual preferential treatment depends on rules of origin, product eligibility and compliant documentation; they do not automatically eliminate every border cost.

On exports, briefly: Ghana, Côte d’Ivoire and Nigeria continue to have material commodity-export exposure, including cocoa, gold and crude oil, while cashew, shea, rubber, timber and bauxite matter across different West African producers. This run did not verify current shipment volumes or port throughput for those products. Exporters should distinguish customs-cleared tonnage from targets and reported production, and verify buyer specifications, traceability and destination-market rules before committing supply.

## Commercial Opportunity
The clearest testable angle is a **buyer-backed potato supply and post-harvest pilot in Côte d’Ivoire**, prompted by the reported 45% import increase. Before planting or investing in cold storage, obtain monthly import/wholesale data and quotations from at least three importers or processors; secure a trial offtake specification; then compare delivered local cost with the importer’s landed cost, including grading, storage losses, finance and transport. A suitable first metric is the share of a buyer’s weekly requirement met at equal quality and a competitive delivered price. If that case does not clear, an import-planning service for Ghanaian food distributors—combining FX-aware landed-cost modelling, clearance milestones and buffer-stock triggers—is lower-capital and immediately testable.

## Watch List
- **Ghana rice permits:** Check for a formal instrument and the exact local-production conditions; policy headlines alone are not a customs instruction.
- **Tema versus Abidjan delays:** Seek current forwarder quotes and measured clearance/dwell data before rerouting cargo.
- **Côte d’Ivoire potato imports:** Confirm the reported growth against official trade statistics, absolute tonnage, seasonality and buyer demand.
- **FX and trade finance:** Track settlement availability and all-in financing cost, not only the headline exchange rate.
- **Regional preferences:** Confirm AfCFTA/ECOWAS rules of origin and product eligibility for each shipment.
