## Commercial Opportunity

**Best risk-adjusted angle: buyer-backed aggregation and quality assurance, not speculative farming or commodity trading.** Start in one Ghanaian producing district and one crop with an identified buyer—cashew, cocoa derivatives or shea depending on local supply. Secure written grade, moisture, packaging and delivery requirements first; map producer groups; then pilot weighing, drying, sorting, lot traceability and short-duration storage. Earn through a disclosed service margin or contracted handling fee, and only extend farmer advances against verified offtake.

This is more executable than building a large processing plant before feedstock and sales are secured. Measure rejection rate, shrinkage, delivered volume, cycle time, gross margin per tonne and cash-conversion days. If demand is proven, add modular processing or solar-assisted storage. In parallel, explore irrigation-as-a-service only where water rights, seasonal demand, power economics and paying anchor farmers have been validated. Unit economics remain unvalidated until local quotations and buyer terms are obtained.
