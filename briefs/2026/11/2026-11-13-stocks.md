# 📈 IPOs & Stock Market Watch — Friday, 13 November 2026

## Headline Trends

The key fresh event is Kenya's Quickmart debut on the Nairobi Securities Exchange. Multiple market reports dated 12 November say the shares rose 18.7% to KSh8.90 on listing; reported valuation was KSh35.6 billion. The reported oversubscription and day-one gain are encouraging, but they are not a substitute for the exchange's final allotment and offer statistics.

Ghana's market remains a regional bright spot in recent reporting: the GSE reportedly recorded a 63% gain in October, while transactions in the year to October were reported up 365%. These are cumulative-period claims, not today's return. A 11 November market report noted GCB Bank down 4.1% as the composite index eased, while a separate report recorded SIC Insurance up 10%—evidence of stock-level dispersion rather than a uniform rally.

The BRVM homepage's live board was marked session closed at 13 November, 01:15 GMT. Its displayed stock moves included gains in SLBC (+7.36%), PRSC (+4.67%) and SOGC (+3.07%), alongside declines in BNBC (-3.90%), BBGC (-3.86%) and SHEC (-3.33%). These are individual ticker changes on the board, not a verified same-day index close. Trinidad's TTSE reported its 12 November close at Composite 1,014.75, up 6.83 points, with All T&T up 10.61 points.

## Sentiment Snapshot

**Mixed, with a constructive bias in selected markets.** Quickmart's listing response and Ghana's reported October performance suggest appetite for credible growth stories and local equities. However, recent GSE single-stock declines and BRVM cross-currents argue against calling this a broad, synchronised bull market. The TTSE's positive latest close adds a modestly supportive Caribbean signal. These are inferences from recent reporting and exchange displays; they do not establish a common regional trend.

## Deep Dive

**Ghana Stock Exchange (GSE):** Ghanaian Times reported a 63% October equity-market gain; MyJoyOnline reported 68 million shares traded in October. 3News reported transactions up 365% year-to-date through October. These headlines point to improving liquidity and attention, but turnover growth can reflect both price appreciation and activity; investors should assess breadth, free float and actual traded value before extrapolating. The GSE's market-report page is the primary place to verify the daily figures; same-day 13 November closing data were not available at research time.

**Nigerian Exchange (NGX):** Recent coverage was two-sided. A 10 November market report described a N2.32 trillion rebound led by Dangote Cement, while a report published 11 November said investors lost N193 billion as equities fell. Another 9 November commentary singled out election risk and Dangote Refinery as year-end drivers. These reports imply sharp rotation and headline sensitivity, not a stable market direction. The official NGX daily-report page could not be retrieved during this check, so the figures are attributed to media/market reporting rather than independently validated exchange data.

**BRVM:** The official BRVM page was accessible and showed a closed-session ticker with materially divergent stock changes. This supports a selective, issuer-specific reading. Do not treat that ticker as a confirmed 13 November trading session; the exchange's own page showed the session closed at the time checked.

**Nairobi Securities Exchange:** Quickmart is the standout listing. Press coverage described an 18.7% jump to KSh8.90 on its first day and a KSh35.6 billion valuation. The issuer is described as a partial exit by private-equity investor Adenia. Treat the debut as a strong initial signal, not proof of durable liquidity or a repeatable valuation template. NSE statistics were current only to 11 November on the official page at time of access.

**Johannesburg Stock Exchange:** Recent reporting noted three actively managed equity ETFs added to the Main Board on 12 November, a small but useful product-expansion signal. Separate coverage said Dangote Cement shelved a planned London listing amid market conditions; this is a reminder that venue choice and valuation expectations can derail cross-border IPO plans. The JSE market-data page blocked automated access, so no current index close is asserted here.

**Caribbean exchanges:** TTSE's official homepage showed its 12 November close: Composite 1,014.75 (+6.83), All T&T 1,434.61 (+10.61), and Cross-Listed 79.28 (+0.39). Activity was uneven: AMCL traded 503,162 shares and rose $2.72 to $44.99; MED gained $3.78 to $43.75 on 365 shares, while WIT fell $0.17 on 330 shares. The Jamaica Stock Exchange's official daily data could not be verified in this run; no unsupported JSE index claim is made. No material fresh ECSE listing or capital raise was corroborated.

**IPOs and capital raises:** Quickmart's NSE debut is the clearest completed equity listing in the current reporting window. The key diligence items now are final offer statistics, allocation, free float, lock-ups and post-listing turnover. No independently verified upcoming or live IPO subscription across the other named markets emerged from the sources checked. The planned Dangote Cement London listing was reportedly shelved, not completed; avoid presenting it as an African IPO. A BRVM–Côte d'Ivoire government cooperation announcement in June 2026 aimed to mobilise diaspora savings, but the reviewed public information did not establish a newly launched, investable diaspora product or current terms.

## Commercial Opportunity

The most actionable angle is not speculative IPO chasing; it is the **investor-access and issuer-readiness gap**. A regulated platform or broker partnership could package regional listed securities for diaspora investors with clear KYC, local-currency/FX pricing, custody, dividend handling, tax disclosures and realistic liquidity warnings. The BRVM's stated diaspora-mobilisation initiative provides a partnership lead, not evidence that a product is already available. In parallel, Quickmart's debut gives Kenyan mid-sized consumer businesses a fresh reference point for public-market readiness—but only where audited reporting, governance, free float and ongoing investor communications are credible.

For a Ghana-based operator, a sensible pilot is a broker-led diaspora education and onboarding service rather than an unlicensed trading venue: measure verified accounts, funded accounts, average first deposit, FX/custody cost and 90-day retention before building proprietary infrastructure.

## Watch List

- **Quickmart:** official final offer/allotment data, stabilisation, turnover and whether the first-day premium holds.
- **GSE:** daily breadth, traded value and whether October's reported activity persists beyond a strong month.
- **NGX:** follow-through after the reported rebound and subsequent loss; energy and election narratives may continue to drive rotation.
- **BRVM diaspora initiative:** look for an actual product announcement, licensed distributor, subscription terms and investor protections before treating it as investable.
- **Caribbean liquidity:** TTSE's rising index points alongside low volumes in many names; watch whether gains are supported by broader turnover.
