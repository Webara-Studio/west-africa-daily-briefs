## Deep Dive

### Projects and tenders

No new project announcement or tender could be independently confirmed for today across Ghana, Nigeria, Côte d’Ivoire, Senegal or ECOWAS. Developers should monitor official procurement portals and regulator notices rather than rely on recycled project headlines. The regional project opportunity is still concentrated in decentralised supply, rooftop and C&I solar, mini-grids and the enabling grid infrastructure needed to connect variable generation.

### Reform and policy

The commercial issue is not simply whether a country has a renewable-energy target; it is whether licensing, tariff-setting, net-metering, interconnection and payment rules permit a project to earn predictable cash flow. In Ghana, check the Energy Commission and PURC for live rules and tariff notices before quoting customers. In Nigeria, DARES creates a route for private mini-grid and stand-alone solar providers, but the developer must validate the active application windows and subsidy mechanics with programme and government sources.

### Storage and off-grid

Storage is commercially strongest when it solves a measurable customer problem: diesel displacement, peak management, continuity for refrigeration or production, or a mini-grid’s evening supply gap. The practical offer is a sized solar-plus-battery system with monitoring, maintenance and a clear service-level commitment—not a battery sale detached from customer economics. Pay-as-you-go household solar can widen access, but collections, customer acquisition and local-currency financing make unit economics decisive.

### Investment and finance

The World Bank’s Nigeria DARES commitment is a significant established financing framework, not a new financing close today. AfDB and other development-finance institutions remain important sources for grid, access and renewable-energy programmes. Investors should distinguish committed programme envelopes from funds contracted to a specific project, and signed financing from cash disbursed. The same discipline applies to green-bond claims: verify issuance, use-of-proceeds allocation and project-level deployment.

## Commercial Opportunity

**Best near-term angle: solar-plus-storage as a managed energy service for high-outage-cost businesses.** Start in one dense market—such as Accra or Lagos—and target cold-chain operators, clinics, hotels, light manufacturers and larger retailers with documented generator fuel bills. Offer an energy audit and a small pilot, then price against verified diesel and outage savings through a lease, power-purchase agreement or service contract.

The advantage is direct customer value and a shorter route to revenue than waiting for a large utility-scale tender. The main risks are customer credit, imported-equipment and FX exposure, battery replacement assumptions, and interconnection or licensing requirements. Mitigate them with a credit screen, conservative degradation and replacement modelling, local-currency pricing where feasible, and written clarity on permits and grid interfaces. For rural projects, pursue mini-grid opportunities only with a credible anchor load, productive-use plan and confirmed subsidy eligibility.

## Watch List

- Nigeria DARES: live procurement notices, developer eligibility, subsidy disbursement and delivery data.
- Ghana: PURC tariff determinations, Energy Commission licensing guidance, net-metering and interconnection rules.
- Côte d’Ivoire and Senegal: official utility and energy-ministry tenders, especially solar-plus-storage and grid-connection packages.
- West African Power Pool and ECOWAS: cross-border transmission and regional market developments that could improve renewable integration.
- Project finance quality: actual financial close, construction progress, commissioning and customer collections—not announced capacity alone.
