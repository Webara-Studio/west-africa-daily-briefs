A credible pilot comparison needs matched origin amount, all-in fees and FX spread, net recipient payout, settlement time, failure rate, liquidity/prefund cost and legal route. No corridor savings claim should be made until those are measured.

## Commercial Opportunity

The most actionable opportunity is **regulated payout and reconciliation infrastructure**, not launching another retail token or exchange. Start with one narrow corridor (UK→Ghana is a sensible test), an appropriately authorised exchange/payment partner and a licensed local payout institution. Offer transparent quotes, transaction monitoring, treasury limits, exception handling and auditable reconciliation from stablecoin settlement to GHS payout.

Run a small, permissioned pilot and compare net recipient proceeds and delivery reliability against an incumbent remittance route. Measure cost per successful payout, time-to-finality, payout failure rate, prefunding days and compliance exceptions. Do not hold customer funds or market a crypto transfer service until Ghanaian and origin-country counsel confirms the permitted structure. The chief risks are licensing ambiguity, depegging/redemption exposure, fraud and sanctions screening, FX slippage, bank/MoMo payout interruption and inadequate liquidity.

## Watch List

- **Ghana:** New BoG/SEC notices, sandbox cohort changes and any formal transition to full activity-specific VASP authorisation.
- **e-Cedi:** Primary-source evidence of a live pilot, participant expansion or production launch; distinguish technical testing from public availability.
- **Nigeria:** Current SEC register and CBN notices; verify named legal entities and allowed activities, not just brand names.
- **Kenya/South Africa:** Implementation and enforcement notices that may offer useful regional compliance precedents.
- **Corridor economics:** Published, comparable UK→Ghana and US→Nigeria prices, including FX spread and final payout, rather than network fees alone.
