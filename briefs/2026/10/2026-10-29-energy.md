# ⚡ Renewable Energy & Power — Thursday, 29 October 2026

## Headline Trends

- **Côte d’Ivoire — project finance:** AXIAN Energy has secured up to €55.5 million (about US$62.34 million) from FMO for the 50 MWp Kong solar plant. The project is expected to generate 96 GWh a year from 2028, with output contracted to CI-ENERGIES under a 25-year agreement. The financing is a meaningful bankability signal, but it is a financing agreement for a project still under development—not operating capacity.
- **Ghana — solar procurement and storage:** At an ECOWAS energy ministers’ meeting in Accra, Ghana’s energy minister said competitive bidding had been completed for approximately 200 MW of solar, with another 200 MW planned. He also cited seasonal peak/off-peak demand differences of as much as 800 MW and the need for storage to improve peak management and use of existing generation.
- **Regional rules — storage and market integration:** ECOWAS ministers are considering a regional directive on battery energy storage systems and an amendment to the Regional Electricity Market Levy. These proposals aim to support variable renewables and fund regional institutions; the reports describe instruments under consideration, not finalised law.
- **Asset lifecycle — waste management:** Recent coverage flags growing pressure to regulate end-of-life solar panels, batteries and inverters. Collection, safe handling and recycling may become a commercial and compliance requirement as deployment expands.

## Sentiment Snapshot

**Bullish, with execution caveats.** The strongest near-term signals are a large, long-tenor financed project in Côte d’Ivoire, Ghana’s stated procurement pipeline and a regional policy discussion that explicitly links batteries with solar integration. The positive story is about pipeline formation and enabling infrastructure—not a sudden increase in commissioned capacity. Bankability, utility payment risk, grid readiness, procurement transparency and currency exposure remain material constraints. No newly verified tariff adjustment or utility reform is evidenced in the sources reviewed for this brief.

## Deep Dive

**1. Solar and project finance.** Kong illustrates the structure developers and financiers can take to market: a defined 50 MWp plant, an identified utility buyer, a 25-year power sale arrangement and long-term debt from a development finance institution. AXIAN’s announced project-company structure includes Africa Via and PIDG’s InfraCo. The reported 2028 operating target provides a useful milestone, but developers and suppliers should validate land, permits, grid interconnection, EPC scope, payment security and financial-close conditions before treating the project as executable demand.

**2. Ghana’s procurement signal.** The minister’s reported 200 MW completed competitive bidding and a further 200 MW planned could create opportunities for developers, EPC contractors, equipment suppliers, grid engineers and operations providers. The announcement alone does not establish the identity of winning bidders, signed PPAs, final capacity allocation, payment terms or tender timetable for the next tranche. Suppliers should qualify through formal procurement channels rather than build inventory against an unawarded pipeline.

**3. Storage and regional market reform.** Ghana’s reported peak/off-peak gap makes the case for dispatchable flexibility, but a battery business case depends on the value stack: peak shaving, ancillary services, avoided diesel, renewable firming and the applicable market/payment rules. The proposed ECOWAS BESS instrument could improve alignment across jurisdictions; the proposed levy amendment may strengthen the institutions needed for a functioning regional market. Neither proposal should be priced into a project as an effective rule until adoption, implementation and revenue arrangements are confirmed.

**4. Off-grid and investment.** The sources checked for this edition did not establish a fresh West African mini-grid award, PAYGo funding round or green-bond issuance with enough detail to report as a new transaction. That is not evidence that no activity exists; it is a limit of this day’s source set. The region’s access gap and unreliable supply continue to support decentralised solar, productive-use systems and storage, but commercial selection should be driven by verified customer demand, collections and service logistics rather than headline access statistics alone.

**5. Regulation and policy discipline.** This scan found a current regional regulatory discussion and announced procurement/finance activity, but no sufficiently verified new national tariff order, IPP licensing change or fossil-fuel phase-out commitment to claim today. Investors should check the relevant regulator and ministry notices, procurement documents and grid-operator requirements country by country before making investment decisions.

## Commercial Opportunity

The most actionable angle is **solar-plus-storage integration and delivery services tied to real procurement or customer offtake**, rather than speculative ownership of a large plant. In Ghana, prepare to compete for or subcontract into the stated solar procurement pipeline, and develop storage-control and grid-integration capability that can respond to the peak-demand problem. In Côte d’Ivoire, the Kong deal validates utility-scale solar finance and creates a route for qualified local suppliers and service firms, subject to the project’s actual procurement schedule.

For a lean Ghanaian operator, a prudent first move is to build a bankable pilot for a factory, cold chain, hotel or telecom site: audit load and outage costs; size PV and batteries against interval data; agree a cedi-denominated or properly hedged service contract; specify battery warranties and end-of-life handling; and secure a credible payment mechanism. Do not quote savings until the site’s load profile, tariff, backup-fuel costs and financing assumptions have been measured. MoMo can simplify collections for smaller customers, but does not remove credit risk or the need for maintenance coverage.

## Watch List

- Publication of Ghana’s formal tender documents, award results, PPAs and delivery schedule for the reported 200 MW solar procurement; clarification of the planned additional 200 MW.
- Final adoption and national implementation of the proposed ECOWAS battery-storage directive and Regional Electricity Market Levy amendment.
- Kong Solar’s permitting, financial-close conditions, EPC and grid-connection milestones, and progress towards expected 2028 operations.
- Whether Ghana publishes a bankable storage procurement or remuneration framework that rewards flexibility, rather than relying on an announcement of need alone.
- Country-level rules for battery and PV equipment take-back, recycling and safe disposal as installed fleets grow.
