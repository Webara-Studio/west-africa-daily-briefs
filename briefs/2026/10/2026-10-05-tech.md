## Deep Dive

### 1. Merchant MoMo bookkeeper and payment reconciliation

**Problem hypothesis:** Small retailers, food vendors and service businesses may receive payments through multiple MoMo wallets and struggle to reconcile transaction notifications against sales, outstanding customer credit and daily cash. The first product should not hold funds or attempt to replace provider wallets. It should make existing transactions easier to understand.

**MVP:** A lightweight Android app plus WhatsApp entry point for daily sales, customer tabs, expense capture and receipt/transaction-reference matching. Start with merchant-entered records or consent-based statement import; do not promise automatic access to provider data until integrations and permissions are confirmed. Add USSD only for a narrowly defined action if a telco/aggregator route is viable.

**Commercial model:** Free or low-cost single-shop tier to acquire usage; paid monthly multi-user/branch reporting; optional bookkeeping/export services through a vetted accountant or merchant association. Validate willingness to pay before setting a price.

**Local fit and risks:** MoMo is central to everyday payments, but the product must accommodate different providers, intermittent data, shared handsets, low digital confidence and privacy expectations. A ledger that is useful offline and syncs when connected is more credible than a data-heavy dashboard. Obtain explicit user consent, minimise stored transaction data, and avoid representing the tool as a regulated payment service.

### 2. Offline-first produce collection, weighing and settlement log

**Problem hypothesis:** Aggregators, farmer groups and produce buyers need a reliable record connecting supplier, crop, weight/grade, collection, transport and settlement. Disputes over quantities, delays and payment status are costly; a generic marketplace does not solve those operational frictions.

**MVP:** A field-agent Android/PWA workflow that records farmer and batch details, timestamped weights, photo evidence where useful, collection status and MoMo/cash settlement references. Queue records offline and synchronise later. Support voice prompts and local-language labels after testing the relevant district and languages; do not assume one language set works across Ghana or the four target countries.

**Commercial model:** B2B subscription or per-active-agent fee paid by an aggregator, processor or buyer; implementation/onboarding fee for larger operations. Avoid charging smallholders before the buyer-side value is proven.

**Local fit and risks:** Designed for weak connectivity, low-cost Android devices and MoMo settlement, this can improve accountability without building a speculative open marketplace. The critical gate is securing one committed buyer/aggregator and measuring reduced reconciliation time, rejected loads or payment disputes during a paid pilot.

### 3. Low-bandwidth appointment and follow-up service for local clinics

**Problem hypothesis:** Smaller clinics may need a simpler way to manage appointment reminders, follow-up and basic patient communication where smartphone app adoption is uneven.

**MVP:** An SMS/WhatsApp reminder and clinic-side appointment queue, with an optional USSD route only after partner and regulatory review. Keep clinical records out of a lightweight reminder tool unless appropriate security, consent, clinical governance and data-protection controls are in place.

**Commercial model:** Clinic subscription or per-site service fee; potential sponsored access through an employer, insurer or health programme. Confirm procurement and willingness to pay directly with clinics.

**Local fit and risks:** Low data use and familiar messaging can improve access, but patient data is sensitive and health claims create material risk. This is a slower, more regulated route than merchant bookkeeping; begin with reminders and operational workflow, not diagnosis or telemedicine claims.

## Commercial Opportunity

**Priority: validate idea 1 first; run idea 2 only with a named anchor buyer.** Merchant reconciliation is the narrowest test: interview 15–20 merchants, observe their current end-of-day process, prototype a single-shop ledger and test whether it saves measurable time or prevents missed receipts. No assumption of integration access should be embedded in the initial promise.

For the field product, secure a buyer/aggregator willing to sponsor a four-to-six-week pilot. Track record completeness, time to settle, disputed weights/payments, agent adoption and offline-sync failures. Expand across countries only after a workflow proves portable; language, mobile-money rails, regulation and buyer relationships are country-specific.

Across all three ideas, use small data payloads, local currency, consent-led data handling, an offline-capable core and customer support via channels users already use. Validate payments, pricing and data-protection obligations with local partners before scaling. Do not build an elaborate native app before confirming repeat use and who pays.
