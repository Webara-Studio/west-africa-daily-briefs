## Sentiment Snapshot

**Mixed, with an infrastructure-led tilt.** Stablecoins remain attractive as a potential settlement and treasury tool, but the gap between a cheap blockchain transfer and a dependable, compliant payout into a bank account or MoMo wallet is where the real commercial risk sits. On regulation, Ghana is moving through a formal coordination and sandbox phase; Nigeria's SEC lists Busha and Quidax under its Accelerated Regulatory Incubation Program (ARIP), which should not be described as unrestricted full licensing.

### 2. Regulation: Ghana, Nigeria and comparators

- **Ghana:** The SEC's notice dated **19 August 2026** names virtual-asset sandbox participants. It is evidence of a supervised pilot track, not blanket exchange authorisation. Bank of Ghana search results surface the Virtual Assets Coordinating Committee inauguration, policy material and a virtual-assets market update; no newer, specific e-Cedi launch or pilot outcome was corroborated in this run. The e-Cedi remains a separate CBDC programme and should not be conflated with privately issued stablecoins.
- **Nigeria:** SEC's live FinPort operator page places Busha Digital and Quidax Technologies in ARIP. That status is materially different from full authorisation for every activity. No new CBN VASP rule or material restriction was corroborated in the dated search results retrieved today; operators should check the SEC and CBN notices directly before relying on the current perimeter.
- **Kenya:** BitKE reported publication of 2026 VASP regulations, with a compliance deadline reported for November. Treat this as a regional comparator, not West African policy, and verify the precise commencement and licence requirements against Kenyan primary instruments before acting.
- **South Africa:** Daily Maverick reported an FSCA-related dispute involving Africa Bitcoin Corporation on 19 October. The headline alone does not establish a general crypto restriction; read the regulator's underlying notice and the conduct at issue before drawing a market-wide conclusion. Recent coverage also reported a CASP licence acquisition by Kredete, but this was not independently checked against the FSCA register here.

## Commercial Opportunity

The strongest near-term play is **B2B stablecoin settlement with regulated local payout**, not a speculative token launch. Start with a narrow, repeatable corridor and a licensed payments/FX partner; compare a stablecoin-enabled payout against the customer's incumbent bank or remittance route. Measure total cost per successful payout, time-to-funds, FX slippage, reconciliation exceptions and compliance-review time. Do not custody customer assets or promise redemption without confirmed permissions and counterparties. In Ghana, first establish the applicable SEC/BoG position and sandbox or partner requirements; in Nigeria, verify the activity-specific SEC status rather than relying on ARIP participation alone.

The principal risk is building a technically elegant rail without dependable local liquidity, lawful operating permissions and a reliable cedi/naira exit. Stablecoin depeg/redemption, fraud, sanctions screening, prefunding and bank/MoMo payout failures can erase the network-fee advantage.

## Watch List

- **Ghana SEC/BoG:** New notices after the August sandbox list, exact participant permissions and a dated e-Cedi pilot or launch update.
- **Nigeria:** SEC/CBN circulars and changes to the FinPort directory; distinguish programme admission from full, activity-specific authorisation.
- **Kenya:** Whether the reported November compliance deadline leads to published licence decisions and final implementation guidance.
- **South Africa:** FSCA primary notice and any register entry behind the latest Africa Bitcoin Corporation and CASP reporting.
- **Stablecoin corridors:** Kora deployment details and independently measurable UK→Ghana/US→Nigeria payout economics, not just transaction announcements.
