# West Africa Weekly — Week 30, 2026

West Africa’s commercial story this week was a shift from headline growth to controlled value capture. Export policy, power infrastructure, capital-market access, regulation and regional integration are all moving in the same direction: the opportunity is real, but the winners will be operators who can document, finance and deliver reliably across fragmented markets.

## 1. West Africa’s Export Model Is Being Rewritten Around Processing

Gold and crude remain the region’s largest hard-currency engines, with Ghana reporting record first-half gold export receipts of US$18.2 billion and Nigeria’s crude output reported above 1.7 million barrels per day. But the more consequential development is policy: cocoa producers are converging around reduced raw-bean exports, Nigeria is restricting raw cocoa and extending controls affecting shea, whilst Guinea is moving towards bauxite export curbs after a sharp rise in output and weaker prices.

**Why it matters**

This is a structural repricing of the trade opportunity. Governments are trying to retain more refining, grinding, extraction and manufacturing locally, which creates openings for licensed processors, assayers, aggregators, traceability providers and regional logistics operators — whilst making unhedged bulk trading more exposed to policy shocks. The sensible entry is staged: secure a licensed processor and buyer, produce documented samples, then scale into cocoa, shea or cashew semi-processing rather than committing immediately to heavy plant.

## 2. Solar Is Moving From Project Announcement to Operating Infrastructure

Côte d’Ivoire commissioned the 52 MWp Ferké solar plant in the north, adding another operating utility-scale asset to the Francophone West African market. Ghana’s Buipe solar project is reportedly expected to begin work in August, whilst Nigeria is tightening its decentralised-power framework and applying clearer permitting boundaries to mini-grids above 100 kW. Commercial and industrial installations are also scaling: Daystar Power’s recent West African projects for Nestlé have reached nearly 7 MW.

**Why it matters**

The investment case is broadening beyond panels and one-off engineering contracts. Businesses that solve storage, collections, maintenance, interconnection, productive-use demand and customer credit can earn recurring revenue from unreliable-grid conditions. For Ghanaian and regional operators, the stronger model is energy-as-a-service for agro-processors, telecoms, SMEs and public facilities, supported by local EPC and O&M capability, disciplined power-purchase contracts and careful management of foreign-exchange and battery-replacement risk.

## 3. Ghana’s Equity Rally Is Creating a Distribution Opportunity — Not a Free Lunch

The Ghana Stock Exchange Composite reached 15,118.75 on 22 July, up 72.39% year to date. Kasapreko’s GHS700 million offer was reported as 146% oversubscribed and is now trading, whilst BRVM is also producing significant new issuance, including Bridge Bank Group Côte d’Ivoire’s approximately US$118 million offer. Nairobi is widening its product set with a US-dollar green REIT and derivatives, showing that African exchanges are moving beyond plain local-currency equities.

**Why it matters**

The opportunity is not simply to launch another brokerage application. It is to build regulated distribution for diaspora and smaller regional investors: transparent FX, curated IPO access, pooled or fractional exposure where permitted, research and simple reporting. Ghana is the natural beachhead because of its strong performance, recognisable issuers and diaspora links; BRVM is the logical second market because one regional exchange can reach eight UEMOA jurisdictions. The principal risk is liquidity and valuation: a strong index does not guarantee easy exits.

## 4. ECOWAS–AES Friction Is Turning Compliance Into Core Infrastructure

ECOWAS is attempting to preserve regional economic integration after the Sahel states’ breakaway posture, while advancing practical cooperation such as the proposed clearing-bank arrangement for the West African Power Pool. At the same time, Nigeria is coordinating virtual-asset oversight through a presidential framework, and Guinea’s reported move against raw gold exports reinforces the regional push towards domestic processing and formal export channels. Security pressure is also rising, with terrorism fatalities in West Africa reportedly up 14% year on year in the first half of 2026.

**Why it matters**

For cross-border businesses, the immediate risk is not necessarily a sudden trade stop; it is rising friction in customs, payments, travel documents, insurance, route security and dispute resolution. A company that can map tariffs, certificates of origin, licences, KYC/AML requirements and corridor risk — and maintain auditable records — is selling access through uncertainty, not merely access to Africa. Coastal hubs and regional gateways remain the sensible starting points, with expansion corridor by corridor rather than a single “West Africa” launch assumption.

## 5. Stablecoins Are Becoming Payments Infrastructure, With Regulation Attached

BTC and ETH were broadly steady at approximately US$65,133 and US$1,902, but price action was not the important regional signal. Ghana has moved into implementation of its virtual-asset regime, with 11 firms reported in a 12-month regulatory sandbox, whilst the e-Cedi project remains active around payments and possible cross-border settlement. Nigeria remains the region’s largest demand centre, and market activity is shifting towards compliant stablecoin settlement, remittances and treasury management rather than retail speculation.

**Why it matters**

The investable opportunity is a compliance-first operating layer connecting dollar-denominated digital assets to Ghanaian and Nigerian bank and MoMo payouts. A credible product would combine licensed local partners, USDT/USDC conversion without directional exposure, KYC/KYB, sanctions screening, wallet-risk monitoring, automated reconciliation and transparent receipts. The critical caveat is classification: sandbox participation is not a full licence, and operators should not custody assets, market investment returns or run P2P liquidity before local counsel and the relevant regulators confirm the model.
